# Member 1: Data and baseline implementation

## Sample and sources
We collected 399 SGX weekly flow reports released between 1 January 2019 and 30 September 2026, extracting the four Weekly Top 10 tables (15,960 observations). The collection contains 236 historical source codes. Three reports have unresolved publication dates preceding their derived observation-week end and are excluded as whole reports. The retained sample has 396 reports and 15,840 signals. An unreported name is not assigned a zero flow.

Daily vendor data cover 2016 through September 2026, allowing earlier returns to be supplied to the risk analyst. Candidate histories are available for 170 original codes, including officially supported S51–5E2 and J91U–9A4U code relations. Sixty-six codes have no corresponding candidate history. Two available histories, Y92 and YF8, are quarantined in their entirety: the former has unreconciled adjusted-price jumps, and the latter omits the economic value of an in-kind distribution. Thus 168 original codes remain eligible for price lookup, representing 166 distinct canonical histories. Availability at a particular signal date can still be lower; a successful download does not imply coverage before an IPO or throughout the requested period.

Sources are the SGX report archive and individual report URLs; Yahoo chart responses and their receipt URLs; State Street's official ES3 historical NAV and distribution table; and official code-change announcements recorded in verified_code_lineage.csv. Raw response bytes and checksums are retained. Ordinary code-plus-.SI identity matches remain vendor candidates, not an independently verified historical security master.

## Fixed missing-data and execution rules
The same rules apply to Smart Retail Flow FT5009 and Smart Insti Flow FT5009:

1. Signals are implemented at the first benchmark-session close strictly after the displayed release date. Prior holdings earn that day's return. Publication metadata in today's archive may differ from historical availability; three internally inconsistent reports are excluded rather than assigned invented dates.
2. Each strategy starts with SGD 1 million. Target long and short exposures are each 49% of pre-cost NAV, divided equally across the respective reported lists and subject to a 10% name cap. Missing or quarantined names retain their original allocation as cash; remaining names are not scaled up to replace them. These are target exposures, not daily enforced limits.
3. New listings become tradable only when a valid contemporaneous quote and positive volume exist; earlier returns are not backfilled. A short VaR history does not by itself prevent strategy inclusion. The risk analyst must disclose dates for which the required 500-return window is unavailable.
4. Missing new-entry prices, unavailable history, non-positive execution volume or unavailable borrowing prevent the new trade and are logged. A missing return on an existing holding stops the run; it is never silently treated as zero. There is no invented delisting settlement. Retired securities lacking histories are excluded by the same availability policy; any held security requiring an unavailable terminal valuation would stop the run.
5. An existing holding with zero execution volume is frozen. Its marked exposure reserves the side's allocation before other trades are sized. Retrying occurs at the next observed release; frozen positions may cause unavoidable target deviations. Missing weekly reports imply holding until the next observed usable report.
6. Vendor adjusted-price ratios supply total-return exposure; USD series use a strictly prior dated USD/SGD close, at most five calendar days old. This daily-bar FX convention is a timing approximation. The vendor's split-adjusted price/volume basis is not independently certified, so reported share equivalents and liquidity participation are proxies.
7. Gross runs use no transaction or borrow charges. Net runs charge 20 basis points on absolute traded notional and 3% annual borrow on prior short notional using calendar days/365. Doubled-cost runs use 40 basis points and the same borrow fee. Cash and collateral interest are zero; all securities are assumed borrowable. Fractional synthetic total-return units are used, with no board-lot rounding, market impact, recalls or final forced liquidation.

## Benchmark
ES3 official NAV and issuer distributions produce daily returns (NAV_t + distribution_t)/NAV_(t-1) - 1, reinvested at the ex-date closing NAV. This is an ETF NAV total-return proxy, not the official STI total-return index or ETF executable market-price return. Its reconstructed 2026 return through September is 25.8364%, consistent with the issuer's rounded 25.84%. The 2,698-day 2016–September 2026 NAV calendar matches valid STI index-price dates. ETF expenses and tracking differences remain relevant.

## Exclusions and interpretation
The 66 missing histories are allowed under an explicitly disclosed availability rule; obtaining every code is not a prerequisite for this restricted-sample baseline. Missingness is unlikely to be random: retired, merged and renamed securities may be disproportionately absent, producing availability/survivorship bias and changing actual long/short exposure.

Y92 and YF8 are excluded from both strategies over the entire sample because their source total-return series cannot be reconciled, not because their strategy returns are attractive or unattractive. This is a **retrospective source-quality restriction**, decided using a full-sample audit; it must not be described as a point-in-time investable-universe screen. Even with correctly lagged trades, exclusion selection can affect the reported comparison. The resulting results describe this restricted sample and do not establish unbiased full-universe or live-trading performance. An unrestricted point-in-time result would require reliable event and return reconstruction.

The absolute 50% daily-return threshold is a review flag only. Other flagged observations are retained, not clipped, set to zero or automatically removed. Neither absence of an active flag nor a passed arithmetic audit certifies all corporate actions. Member 3 should review remaining flagged historical asset returns before final VaR: even an event occurring outside a held position can enter a later 500-day asset-return window. Source name/code conflicts remain documented and source codes take priority without speculative correction.

## Verification and handoff
All six paths (two strategies times three cost assumptions) are independently reconciled against prior signed holdings, asset returns, cash, transaction fees and borrow fees. Six synthetic engine tests cover publication lag, P&L and fees, missing held prices, unavailable shorting, weekend borrowing and frozen holdings with allocation reservation. Raw JSON checksums, unique positive SGD inputs, benchmark reconstruction and lagged FX are also checked. These checks validate implementation and arithmetic, not independent certification of vendor data.

Member 2 receives daily NAV/returns, holdings, trades, turnover and exclusions. Member 3 receives the aligned asset-return matrix, including pre-2019 observations, and signed holdings; preserve missing histories when forming the preceding 500 aligned returns. Column end_weight/end_value_sgd describes closing positions; start_weight describes prior-close holdings. VaR timing must match the chosen forecast date and must not include a future day's return.

The handoff summary gives only cumulative return, final NAV, drawdown, fees and average exposure as a basic sanity check. Full performance evaluation, enhancement and final risk analysis remain the responsibilities of Members 2 and 3.
